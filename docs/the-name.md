The new official name for Ultima Thule is 486958 Arrokoth. [1] 
The small, icy Kuiper belt object was initially given the provisional designation 2014 MU69 and nicknamed "Ultima Thule" by [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/new-horizons-chooses-nickname-for-ultimate-flyby-target/) prior to its history-making flyby by the New Horizons spacecraft. NASA officially changed the name to Arrokoth after the original nickname drew criticism for its historical ties to Nazi occultism and ideology. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] 
Key facts about Arrokoth:

* 
* Meaning: The word Arrokoth means "sky" in the Powhatan and Algonquian languages. NASA selected it with the consent of Powhatan tribal elders to honor the indigenous people of the region where the object was studied. [4, 6] 
* Shape: High-resolution images revealed that it is a "contact binary," meaning it looks like a flattened snowman formed by two distinct icy lobes gently merging together. [7, 8] 
* Location: It is located in the Kuiper Belt, sitting roughly 1 billion miles beyond Pluto. [2, 9] 
* Significance: Arrokoth remains the most distant cosmic body ever explored by a human spacecraft. [4]
